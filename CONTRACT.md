# Product contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Coaster is expense management that replaces Expensify, SAP Concur Expense or Emburse for a US company of 15–150 employees, with the card, policy-agent and conversational depth buyers now look to Ramp, Navan and Brex for. Receipts are snapped, forwarded, texted or pulled from inboxes and read automatically; corporate-card and bank feeds are matched to receipts; policy is enforced as people spend and submit; approvals chase the people who owe them; reimbursements go out by ACH through a payment provider and are tracked to settlement; and every expense, receipt, reimbursement and clearing entry syncs both ways with QuickBooks Online, Xero or Pantry. Money is USD by default, distance is miles at the IRS standard mileage rate, per diem follows GSA rates, and schedules respect US time zones and business days.

The product is the whole expense lifecycle the incumbents sell: capture, cards and feeds, claims, coding, policy, approval, follow-up, reimbursement, accounting, reporting, administration, mobile and migration from each incumbent. Neighboring products stay separate and are integrated, never rebuilt: Pantry is the ledger, Purse owns supplier purchasing and bills, Willow holds the HR record, Gauge bills clients for project time and expenses, Nook is team chat, Auth is workforce sign-in and Crank runs cross-product automations. Payroll is run by the buyer's payroll provider. No gap in implementation reduces this destination: provider applications, card programs, ACH onboarding and native apps are engineering work recorded as clauses below.

This is a target contract, not a release verdict. Every named clause states the behavior a buyer gets and its acceptance evidence: the journey or test through a public door (screen, HTTP API, agent API, CLI or provider webhook) that proves it. The [Status](#status) section marks each clause done, partial or missing at current code; a cited local test does not establish hosted operation, a live provider connection or an accessibility certification. Existing clause IDs COA-001 to COA-013 are preserved and their flow tests in `tests/coaster.test.mjs` remain their proof; `tests/browser.mjs` drives the same flows through the screens at 1440 and 390 pixels wide. This documentation round changes no homepage, interface or runtime behavior.

## Existing guarantees: the claim-to-reimbursement spine

- **COA-001 — Business, categories and people set by the owner.** The owner names the business and sets its currency (fixed once claims exist), timezone, mileage rate, optional per-diem rate and receipt threshold; adds categories with account code, tax code and optional per-expense limit; and adds people as employee, manager and/or finance with each employee's approving manager. Nobody else can change these. LOCALE-001 sets the US defaults and ADMIN-001 widens the roles. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-001` flow test: the owner configures the business, categories and people through the HTTP API; a manager, finance member and employee are each refused the same writes and nothing changes.

- **COA-002 — Receipt, mileage and per-diem expenses with receipt provenance.** An employee starts a claim and adds purchases with a receipt photo or PDF, mileage (distance × the business rate, rounded half up once) and per-diem days (days × the business rate). Each receipt keeps its exact bytes, SHA-256, size, type (from its own signature), uploader and time. Invalid dates, amounts and files are refused and change nothing. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-002` flow test: upload a JPEG and a PDF, add mileage and per diem, read back exact bytes and hashes, and see malformed dates, amounts and disguised files refused with no write.

- **COA-003 — Exact splits across categories and cost centers.** An employee splits one expense across categories and cost centers; the parts must add up exactly to the expense, and each part becomes its own accounting line. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-003` flow test: split one expense three ways, see a non-adding split refused, and read three coded accounting lines in the batch export.

- **COA-004 — Policy exceptions explained, acknowledged and carried to the books.** Coaster flags policy exceptions (no receipt above the threshold, an expense's total in a category over that category's limit however it is split, the same receipt twice, the same date/merchant/amount as another expense on the claim, another claim or an imported report). Submission needs the employee's explanation for each; the manager must acknowledge them to approve; they stay on the decision, the batch and the accounting CSV. POLICY-001 extends the rules. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-004` flow test: submit an over-limit, receipt-less and duplicate claim, see submission refused without explanations, approval refused without acknowledgment, and the exceptions in the batch and CSV.

- **COA-005 — An exact duplicate is never reimbursed twice.** One database key per expense (its receipt bytes' SHA-256, or without a receipt its employee, date, type, merchant and amount) admits it to one batch only, and a claim holding the same exact expense twice cannot be submitted. FEED-004 extends the same key to card transactions. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-005` flow test: approve two claims carrying the same receipt and see the second refused from any batch; a claim holding the same expense twice cannot be submitted.

- **COA-006 — The assigned approver decides the exact submitted revision.** Submission freezes the claim as a numbered revision and routes it to the employee's approving manager. Only that approver approves, returns (with a reason) or rejects (with a reason) that exact revision; nobody decides their own claim; a stale view is refused. APPROVE-001 extends routing to multi-step workflows, APPROVE-005 lets the approver hold single expenses and approve the rest, and APPROVE-007 adds retraction and unapproval before payment, all without weakening these rules. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-006` flow test: another manager, the employee and a stale revision are refused; the assigned manager's decision is recorded against the exact revision.

- **COA-007 — Corrections never erase evidence.** A returned claim is corrected and resubmitted as the next revision. Earlier revisions, their receipts and decisions stay readable and downloadable; receipts, revisions, decisions and history lines cannot be changed or deleted. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-007` flow test: return, correct with a replacement receipt and resubmit; both revisions, both receipts and every decision remain readable and no route edits or deletes them.

- **COA-008 — Role-scoped access in every handler.** Access follows roles in every API handler, not only in screens: employees see their own claims; approvers see claims routed to them once submitted; finance and the owner see submitted claims; drafts stay private to their employee. Writes need the current native session from the same origin; deactivated people (in Coaster or in their sign-in account) lose access at once, agent keys included; forged fields in requests decide nothing. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-008` flow test: each role reads and writes across every claim, batch and receipt route; cross-origin, deactivated and forged requests are refused with no write.

- **COA-009 — Reviewed reimbursement batches with reconciled exports.** Finance gathers approved claims into a draft batch, reviews it by confirming the reconciled total (someone else must review a batch holding their own claim), then downloads an accounting CSV (one line per category split with account/tax codes, receipt SHA-256, exceptions and approver) and a reimbursements CSV (one total per person). Both add up to the batch total. Finance can record a payment made outside Coaster with its reference and date; PAY-001 is the provider-paid route and ACCT-001 to ACCT-003 the live ledger route, and this file route stays available alongside them. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-009` flow test: batch, review, download both CSVs, check both sum to the batch total, record the payment and see each employee's claim marked reimbursed.

- **COA-010 — Personal agents prepare; people decide.** A person makes an agent key. Their agent prepares draft claims from receipts (employees) or batch proposals (finance) through `/api/coaster/agent/*`; it cannot submit, approve, batch, pay or read anything else. Only that person accepts or declines; acceptance is recorded with who and when, and the claim or batch says it was prepared by the agent. The push-further agents (AGENT-001 to AGENT-009) build on this authority model. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-010` flow test: an agent key proposes a claim and a batch, is refused submission, approval and foreign reads, and only its person accepts; the provenance appears on the result.

- **COA-011 — Expensify report history move-in.** The owner moves in Expensify history from a report-template CSV (IMPORTING.md) on Base import-kit: preview writes nothing and lists errors; commit applies only the exact previewed bytes and mapping; the original file is retained; undo reverses the job, and the same file can be imported again after an undo. Imported reports stay read-only history and flag later re-claims as possible duplicates. MOVE-001 extends this to the whole Expensify account. **Acceptance evidence:** `COA-011` flow test: preview with errors, commit, see a re-claim flagged, undo and re-import the same file.

- **COA-012 — Whole-installation export and restore.** The owner exports the whole installation with `rer export` and restores it into an empty installation with `rer import`: claims, revisions, decisions, receipt bytes, batches, history and imported history come back identical, and a corrupt archive or one with inconsistent claims is refused without writing. Provider connections and secrets added by later clauses are excluded and must be reconnected (SEC-001). **Acceptance evidence:** `tests/browser.mjs` archive section and `tests/browser-archive.mjs`: export, refuse tampered and inconsistent archives, import into a second empty installation and compare.

- **COA-013 — Fresh-clone install and runtime.** Coaster installs and runs from a fresh clone with `rer install`, `setup`, `dev`, `build`, `preview`, `verify`, `reset`, `export`, `import`, `deploy --dry-run`, `describe` and `check`, on TanStack Start, Hono, D1 and Cloudflare Workers. **Acceptance evidence:** `tests/browser.mjs`, `rer check` and `rer verify` on the exact commit.

## Target clauses

### US defaults

- **LOCALE-001 — US-first defaults.** A new installation defaults to USD, a US time zone chosen at setup, miles, US date and number formats and a receipt threshold of $75 (the IRS documentary-evidence line; lodging always needs a receipt). The mileage rate is an effective-dated table seeded with the IRS standard business mileage rate for each year, editable by the owner, and each mileage expense takes the rate in force on its trip date. Receipts carry sales tax as its own field mapped to a tax code. Other currencies and kilometers remain available per entity (ADMIN-004). **Acceptance evidence:** Set up a fresh installation and see USD, miles, a US zone and the $75/lodging rule; enter mileage dated either side of a January rate change and see each priced at its year's rate; capture a receipt's sales tax and see it on its own accounting line.

### Capture

- **CAPTURE-001 — Photo, file and bulk receipt upload.** On phone or desktop an employee photographs a receipt with the camera or uploads JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC or multi-page PDF files up to 10 MB each, one at a time or dozens at once by drag and drop. Each upload lands in the employee's receipt inbox with its processing state visible (received, reading, read, needs attention) and keeps COA-002 provenance; large files live in R2 with their hash in D1. **Acceptance evidence:** At 390 px photograph a receipt; at 1440 px drop 30 mixed files including an 8 MB three-page hotel folio PDF and a HEIC; see each appear in the inbox with state, exact bytes and hash, and a 12 MB file refused with a reason.

- **CAPTURE-002 — Email-in receipts.** Each installation has a receipts address. Mail from a member's known address (or an alias they confirm) becomes receipts in that member's inbox: attachments, inline images and HTML receipt bodies rendered to a stored PDF. Mail from unknown senders is held for the owner, never attached to anyone. Forwarded receipts keep the original message headers as provenance. **Acceptance evidence:** Forward an airline HTML receipt, a PDF invoice and a photo from a member's address and see three inbox receipts with the source message retained; send one from an unknown address and see it held; confirm an alias and see the next one land.

- **CAPTURE-003 — Automatic receipt reading with a correction path.** Every receipt is read for merchant, date, total, subtotal, sales tax, tip, currency, payment card last four and line items, each with a confidence and the image region it came from. Read fields prefill the expense; low-confidence or conflicting fields are marked for the employee, who corrects them in place, and the correction and the original reading are both retained. A field is shown as confident only when the reader's confidence clears the installation's threshold and it agrees with every other source (card charge, email receipt, line-item sum); anything else is marked for review and never silently prefilled as settled. Reading runs through the Base AI module with an optional Amazon Textract AnalyzeExpense adapter, and failures show an actionable state rather than a silent blank. Reading quality is measured, not asserted: a committed, labeled corpus of at least 40 synthetic US receipts (`tests/fixtures/receipts/` with one label file per receipt giving merchant, date, total, subtotal, sales tax, tip, currency and card last four) covers restaurant receipts with handwritten and printed tips, hotel folios, faded thermal receipts, airline and ride-share email receipts, multi-page PDF invoices, crumpled and skewed photos and a foreign-currency receipt, and every reader adapter is scored against it. **Acceptance evidence:** Run the reading suite over the labeled corpus through the public upload door: (1) every field the reader marks confident equals its label exactly (zero confident-wrong fields); (2) every field that differs from its label is marked for review, never prefilled as confident; (3) each prefilled expense's amount equals the labeled total before the employee touches it whenever the total was marked confident, including the tipped restaurant receipt (total includes the tip), the hotel folio (folio balance, not the first night) and the faded thermal receipt; (4) the per-field read-without-correction rate for merchant, date and total is reported and recorded with the commit, and merchant, date and total are read correctly without review on at least 90% of the corpus. Then correct the faded total and see both values in history; force the reader to fail and see the needs-attention state with a retry.

- **CAPTURE-004 — Receipt inbox and automatic attachment.** Unattached receipts wait in the employee's inbox and are attached automatically to the matching card transaction (FEED-004) or turned into a draft out-of-pocket expense when no card charge matches within a configurable window. The employee can attach, detach, merge duplicates or discard with a reason; discarded receipts stay in history. **Acceptance evidence:** Upload a receipt before its card charge arrives and see it attach when the feed posts; upload a cash receipt and see a draft expense after the window; discard one and find it in history.

- **CAPTURE-005 — Inbox retrieval from Gmail and Outlook.** With an employee's consent, Coaster connects to their Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 mailbox with read-only scopes limited to receipt searches, finds receipts for their unmatched card transactions and attaches them, naming the message each came from. The employee can review what was read and disconnect at any time; nothing outside matching receipt messages is stored. **Acceptance evidence:** Against provider test tenants, connect a mailbox, post an unmatched card charge, see the matching receipt message found and attached with its source; disconnect and see the grant revoked and no further reads.

- **CAPTURE-006 — Receipts by Slack, Microsoft Teams and text message.** An employee sends a receipt photo to the Coaster app in Slack or Teams, or texts it to the installation's US number, and it lands in their inbox; Coaster replies in the same thread with what it read and any question it needs answered. Unknown senders are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Through Slack and Teams test workspaces and a Twilio test number, send a receipt each way and see it in the inbox with a threaded reply of the read fields; reply with the business purpose and see it on the expense; an unlinked Slack user is refused.

- **CAPTURE-007 — Merchant receipt feeds.** The owner connects Uber for Business, Lyft Business and Amazon Business; their trip and order receipts arrive automatically with business purpose and project fields where the merchant provides them, and match to the card charge. **Acceptance evidence:** With provider sandbox data, connect each merchant, post a ride, a trip and an order and see receipts arrive, match their card charges and carry the merchant's expense code.

### Cards and feeds

- **FEED-001 — Card and bank connections through Plaid.** Finance connects company card and bank accounts, and employees connect personal cards for reimbursable spend, through Plaid Link. Transactions arrive through Plaid's incremental sync cursor, assigned to cardholders by account and last four, including pending-to-posted transitions and reversals without duplicates. **Acceptance evidence:** In Plaid sandbox connect a company card and a personal card, run sync through added, modified (pending→posted) and removed transactions and see one expense per posted charge assigned to its cardholder; replay the same cursor and see no duplicates.

- **FEED-002 — Commercial card program feeds and statement import.** For corporate cards from American Express, Visa and Mastercard issuers, Coaster ingests the program's commercial data feed (Amex GL1025, Visa VCF through Visa IntelliLink, Mastercard CDF3 through Smart Data) with no person downloading or uploading files. Transport: each installation gets a dedicated SFTP drop (an inbound SFTP endpoint per installation with its own username and SSH public-key authentication, files PGP-encrypted to the installation's published key) that the issuer pushes to, or, where the issuer only offers pick-up, Coaster pulls from the issuer's SFTP host with a key it holds; either way a scheduled job checks the drop on a cadence finance sets (hourly by default) and imports every new file once, keyed by file name, issuer sequence number and content hash, so a re-delivered file never double-imports. Enrollment is guided: Coaster shows finance the exact values the issuer's commercial-data authorization form asks for (delivery host, port, username, public key, PGP key, the company's program and control account numbers) and a checklist per issuer, records the date each step was done, and marks the feed live when the first file arrives. Each feed has an expected-delivery calendar (daily, or the issuer's business days); a file that has not arrived by its expected time plus a grace period finance sets raises FEED-003's stale-feed alert. Card accounts in each file are assigned to cardholders by account and last four, and new cards in a file are offered to finance for assignment. CSV, OFX and QFX statement files remain the recovery path; any file import is previewed, deduplicated against feed history and undoable on import-kit. **Acceptance evidence:** Against a test SFTP server standing in for the issuer at the network edge, complete the enrollment checklist and see the delivery values; place PGP-encrypted GL1025, VCF and CDF3 fixture files at the drop and, with nobody uploading, see the scheduled job pick each up and cardholders see their transactions once; switch the feed to pull mode and see a file fetched from the issuer host; re-deliver the same GL1025 file and see nothing imported twice; skip the next day's file and see the stale-feed alert (FEED-003) to finance and the spend report show that date as uncovered; import a QFX statement for the same period and see overlaps flagged as duplicates in preview, and an undo reverse the job.

- **FEED-003 — Visible feed health and reconnection.** Every connection shows last successful sync, transaction freshness and the provider's error in plain words (for example a login-required error) with the exact reconnect step; every file-delivered commercial feed (FEED-002) also shows its last file, its next expected file and any missed delivery dates. A stale or broken feed, or a commercial file that missed its expected delivery, alerts its owner and finance (NOTIFY-001) and is never treated as zero spend: reports show the uncovered dates until the late file or backlog arrives. **Acceptance evidence:** Trigger a Plaid login-required error in sandbox; see the connection marked broken with its last sync and a reconnect action, the owner notified, and the spend report show the uncovered dates; reconnect through update mode and see the backlog arrive. With a clock-controlled installation, let a daily GL1025 file miss its expected time plus grace; see the missed date on the feed, the alert to finance and the uncovered date in the report; deliver the late file and see the date covered and the alert closed.

- **FEED-004 — Card transaction expenses and receipt matching.** Each card charge becomes an expense owned by its cardholder, with merchant, amount, date and merchant category. Receipts match charges by amount (allowing tips and currency conversion within set tolerances), date window, merchant and card last four; ambiguous matches ask the employee instead of guessing. Matched pairs can be undone. A card charge and a reimbursable claim for the same purchase cannot both be paid (COA-005's key extended to card transactions). **Acceptance evidence:** Post 20 sandbox charges and 20 receipts including a tipped meal, a foreign charge and two same-amount charges on one day; see 18 auto-matches, two match questions, an undo, and a duplicate out-of-pocket claim for a matched card charge blocked from reimbursement.

- **FEED-005 — Card statement reconciliation and liability clearing.** Finance reconciles each card statement period: Coaster totals posted charges, payments and credits against the statement balance, lists differences, and posts card expenses to the card liability account and the statement payment as its clearing entry (ACCT-001 to ACCT-003). **Acceptance evidence:** Reconcile a fixture statement with one missing charge and one refund; see the difference listed, resolve it, close the period and see the ledger card account match the statement balance.

- **FEED-006 — Individual-liability corporate cards.** For corporate cards where the employee is billed and pays the issuer (individual liability), card charges arrive through the same feeds (FEED-001, FEED-002) marked individual-liability; approved business charges are reimbursed to the employee (PAY-001, PAY-003) instead of posted to a company card liability, personal charges are excluded from reimbursement, and an option pays approved amounts directly to the card account through the payment provider where the issuer accepts it. The ledger posting treats them as reimbursable expenses, not company card spend. **Acceptance evidence:** Mark a fixture card program individual-liability; post six charges, two marked personal; approve the claim and see the four business charges in the reimbursement batch to the employee, the two personal charges excluded, and the QuickBooks sandbox posting as a bill to the employee rather than a purchase on a card account.

- **CARD-001 — Company cards through an issuing partner.** Finance issues virtual and physical Visa or Mastercard cards to employees through a licensed issuing partner (Stripe Issuing or Lithic adapter) with per-card and per-category limits, merchant category allow and block lists, expiring single-use cards and freeze or cancel in one action. Authorizations are approved or declined in real time against the card's policy, the decline reason is shown to the cardholder, and issued-card transactions flow into FEED-004 without a separate feed. **Acceptance evidence:** In the issuing partner's test mode, issue a virtual card with a $500 monthly limit and a blocked category; authorize an allowed purchase, see a blocked-category and an over-limit decline with reasons, freeze the card and see the next authorization declined.

- **CARD-002 — Personal spend on company cards.** A cardholder marks a card charge personal; Coaster records what they owe, collects it by ACH debit through the payment provider or by deduction from their next reimbursement, and tracks it to settlement. Finance can require repayment within a set period and freeze an issued card while a repayment is overdue. **Acceptance evidence:** Mark a sandbox charge personal, repay by netting against a reimbursement and by ACH debit; see both settle and clear the balance; leave one overdue and see the reminder and, for an issued card, the freeze.

### Claims

- **CLAIM-001 — US mileage.** Mileage is entered as miles from start and end addresses, with the driving distance calculated by a routing provider, round-trip and multi-stop options, an optional commute deduction, an odometer photo option and GPS-tracked trips from the mobile app (MOBILE-002). Each trip stores route, distance source and the IRS rate in force (LOCALE-001). **Acceptance evidence:** Enter a three-stop round trip from addresses and see the calculated miles and route; apply a commute deduction; record a GPS trip on the mobile build; see each priced at its date's rate and the distance source retained.

- **CLAIM-002 — GSA per diem by location.** Per diem uses the GSA CONUS rates (and the State Department OCONUS rates) by city, county and fiscal year for meals and incidental expenses and for lodging, with 75% on the first and last travel day, deductions for provided meals, and an option for a company rate or the IRS high-low method. Rate tables update each fiscal year and the rate used is copied onto the expense. **Acceptance evidence:** Claim a four-day trip to Chicago with a provided lunch; see M&IE at the location's rate, 75% on travel days and the lunch deducted; claim a trip spanning 1 October and see each day at its fiscal year's rate.

- **CLAIM-003 — Foreign-currency expenses.** An expense in another currency records the original amount and currency, converts to USD at the transaction-date rate from a named rate provider, and is replaced by the card's posted USD amount when a card charge matches. The rate, source and date are visible and exported. **Acceptance evidence:** Claim a €48.00 Paris taxi and see the USD amount with rate and date; match a EUR receipt to its card charge and see the card's amount win with both kept.

- **CLAIM-004 — Cash advances.** An employee requests a cash advance for a trip; once approved and paid (PAY-001), later expenses draw it down and the claim settles the difference: the company pays the balance or the employee returns the excess, tracked to the IRS 120-day return window. **Acceptance evidence:** Approve and pay a $1,000 advance, claim $830 of expenses and see $170 owed back; claim $1,200 and see $200 reimbursed; let a return lapse and see it reach the overdue list.

- **CLAIM-005 — Spend requests and trip pre-approval.** An employee requests approval before spending (a trip, a conference, a software purchase) with an estimate; approval routes like a claim (APPROVE-001), and later expenses link to the request so approvers see estimate against actual. Requests over a policy threshold are required before booking. Until APPROVE-001's workflows exist, a request routes exactly as a claim does today: to the employee's approving manager, or their APPROVE-002 delegate, and nobody approves their own request; APPROVE-001 must route requests through its workflows before it ships. "Required before booking" means: an expense whose amount is strictly above the owner's "Spend request needed above" amount (integer cents; empty turns the rule off) and that is not linked to an approved request carries a soft pre-approval exception, explained and acknowledged like any COA-004 exception, not a hard block; POLICY-001 adds the option to make it hard. Only an approved request of the same employee can be linked. **Acceptance evidence:** Request a $2,400 conference trip, approve it, then file expenses linked to it and see estimate versus actual on the claim; file an unlinked expense over the threshold and see the pre-approval exception.

- **CLAIM-006 — Billable expenses and client rebilling.** Any expense can be marked billable to a customer and project; billable lines sync to QuickBooks Online or Xero as billable expenses and hand off to Gauge for client invoicing, with their receipts. **Acceptance evidence:** Mark two expenses billable to a customer; see them as billable in the QuickBooks sandbox with attachments and as billable items in a Gauge test installation.

- **CLAIM-007 — Itemization, attendees and required fields.** Hotel folios are itemized into room, tax, meals and incidentals with each part categorized; meals record attendees (employees and guests) and policy can limit per person; the owner defines required custom fields per category (business purpose, client, flight class). A per-person limit divides the expense's total share in that category by the number of attendees listed on it (at least one; the employee is listed like any attendee), rounds each share up to the cent, and raises a soft exception when a share is strictly above the limit. Required fields are checked when the claim is submitted, not on each save, and submission is refused naming every blank field. **Acceptance evidence:** Itemize a three-night folio and see room and in-room dining in their categories; add four attendees to a $320 dinner against a $75 per-person limit and see the exception; leave a required field blank and see submission refused with the field named.

- **CLAIM-008 — Accountable-plan compliance and non-employee claims.** Coaster runs reimbursements as an IRS accountable plan: it tracks substantiation within 60 days of spend and return of excess within 120 days, shows lapses to finance, and routes any amount finance treats as non-accountable to payroll as taxable wages (PAY-003). Contractors can submit claims as non-employees; their reimbursements are flagged for 1099 review in exports. **Acceptance evidence:** File an expense 75 days after the spend and see the lapse flagged; mark it non-accountable and see it in the payroll handoff as taxable; reimburse a contractor and see the 1099 review flag in the export.

- **CLAIM-009 — Recurring expenses and stipends.** Finance sets recurring allowances (for example a $75 monthly phone stipend or a $500 yearly home-office allowance) per person, department or entity, as either a fixed stipend that creates an expense each period without a receipt (accountable or taxable, CLAIM-008) or a reimbursement cap the employee claims against with receipts; employees set recurring personal expenses (a monthly software subscription) that create a draft each period with the receipt requested. Stipends follow approval rules (APPROVE-001, APPROVE-004), stop on deactivation and show remaining allowance to the employee. **Acceptance evidence:** With a clock-controlled installation, set a monthly $75 phone stipend for a department and a yearly $500 home-office cap; advance three months and see three stipend expenses created and reimbursed; claim $320 then $250 against the cap and see the second capped at $180 with the excess flagged; deactivate a member and see their stipend stop.

### Coding

- **CODE-001 — Accounting dimensions from the ledger.** Categories map to the chart of accounts, and classes, locations, departments, projects, customers and vendors are pulled from the connected ledger (ACCT-001 to ACCT-003) and kept current by the scheduled daily refresh, with no one pressing sync; anything archived in the ledger stops being offered. Custom tags and cost centers sit alongside, including dependent multi-level tags (for example department → project → task), where each level offers only the choices allowed under the level above; they are built in the app or imported from CSV, and each level can be required or optional. The owner sets which dimensions each category requires, and each person's default department and location come from ADMIN-002. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a QuickBooks sandbox and see its accounts, classes, locations and customers as choices; add a class in QuickBooks and see it appear after the next scheduled refresh with nobody pressing sync; archive a class and see it no longer offered; import a department → project tag list where Sales offers only Sales projects, and see a Marketing project refused on a Sales expense; require a project on Travel and see a Travel expense refused without one.

- **CODE-002 — Automatic coding.** Coaster fills category, class, location, department, project, customer and memo on every new card charge and receipt from these sources, in this order of precedence: (1) finance's merchant rules (for example "Delta → Airfare, class Sales"), which always win; (2) the employee's own personal rules; (3) learned coding: how this person, then their department, then the company has coded this merchant before, updated after every correction; (4) the card charge's merchant category code, mapped to a category; (5) the receipt reading's merchant and line items (CAPTURE-003). Each suggestion shows the source that produced it and why. A suggestion only fills an empty field and never overwrites coding the employee or an approver has entered. It only uses accounts, classes and tags that are active in the connected ledger at that moment: an archived account or class is never suggested, and a rule that points at one is flagged to finance instead of applied. After a person overrides the coding for a merchant twice, their next charge from that merchant gets the corrected coding. Employees and approvers accept or change each suggestion, and finance sees acceptance rates per rule and per source. Coding quality is measured, not asserted. A committed, labeled coding fixture set (`tests/fixtures/coding/`) holds at least 100 synthetic card charges and receipts, each labeled with category, class and project, plus 12 months of prior coding for the same people and merchants. It covers repeat merchants, merchants new to the company, merchants covered by rules, ambiguous merchants (Amazon, airport shops) and charges whose usual account or class has since been archived. **Acceptance evidence:** Run the coding suite over the fixture set through the public feed and upload doors: (1) every charge a merchant rule covers gets exactly the rule's coding (100%), including where learned history points elsewhere; (2) for repeat merchants (coded at least twice in the history by the person or the company), the suggested category equals the label on at least 90%, and the match rate across all suggestions for category, class and project is reported and recorded with the commit; (3) override one person's coding for a merchant twice and see their third charge from it carry the corrected coding, while another person's charge from the same merchant is unchanged; (4) no suggestion uses an archived account or class, and no suggestion changes a field the employee filled before the charge arrived; (5) open a suggestion and see its source and reason, override it, and see the override and the rule's acceptance rate.

### Policy

- **POLICY-001 — Versioned policy rules.** Finance writes the expense policy as versioned rules with effective dates: limits per expense, per day, per trip, per attendee and per month by category, receipt thresholds, required fields, blocked merchants and categories, weekend and holiday spend, submission deadlines and approval requirements. Each rule is soft (explain and acknowledge, COA-004) or hard (cannot submit). Every exception names the rule version that raised it, and past decisions keep the version they were made under. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish a policy with a daily meal limit, a blocked merchant and a hard 90-day deadline; see each fire with its rule version; publish a new version and see older claims keep the old version and new claims use the new one.

- **POLICY-002 — Duplicate and fraud signals.** Beyond COA-004 and COA-005, Coaster flags the same receipt across different employees, near-duplicate images (cropped or re-photographed), receipts whose image shows edits or generation artifacts, charges split to stay under a limit, round-amount and weekend patterns, and receipts that disagree with the card charge. Each signal shows its evidence; none blocks payment by itself or accuses anyone, and finance resolves it with a recorded reason. Signals are held to a measured false-positive bound so they do not swamp the POLICY-003 queue: a committed clean fixture set of at least 300 legitimate expenses across 20 employees (including recurring same-amount subscriptions, shared team meals split by attendees and legitimately round amounts) raises a signal on no more than 2% of its expenses, and the rate is recorded with the commit. **Acceptance evidence:** Submit the same receipt from two employees, a re-photographed copy, an edited total and two $480 charges against a $500 limit; see each signal with evidence, resolve one as legitimate and see the reason recorded. Run the clean fixture set and see signals on no more than 2% of its expenses, each with its evidence, and the rate recorded.

- **POLICY-003 — Finance exception queue and audit sampling.** Finance sees one queue of open exceptions, signals and lapses across all claims and card spend, sortable by risk and age, and can sample a percentage of approved spend for post-approval audit with findings recorded. **Acceptance evidence:** Generate exceptions across five claims and card charges; see them in one queue ordered by risk; configure a 10% audit sample and see a sampled claim with a recorded finding.

### Approval

- **APPROVE-001 — Multi-step conditional approval workflows.** The owner builds approval workflows from conditions (amount, category, department, project, policy exception, spend request) and steps (approving manager, manager's manager, department head, project owner, named person, finance review), including parallel or sequential steps. Each step keeps COA-006's guarantees: the exact revision, no self-approval, stale views refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure "over $1,000 adds the department head; any exception adds finance review"; submit claims of $200, $1,400 and $300-with-exception and see three different paths; the department head cannot approve their own claim at any step.

- **APPROVE-002 — Delegation, out of office and reassignment.** An approver delegates to a named colleague for a date range; claims route to the delegate and record who approved on whose behalf. Admins reassign stuck approvals, and a deactivated approver's queue moves to their manager automatically. **Acceptance evidence:** Set a delegation for next week and see a claim route to the delegate with "on behalf of" recorded; deactivate an approver and see their queue move with a history line.

- **APPROVE-003 — Approve anywhere.** Approvers decide from the phone (web and native), from a signed one-time link in an email, from interactive Slack and Microsoft Teams messages, and in bulk from the queue; every channel enforces COA-006 and shows the exceptions before the decision. **Acceptance evidence:** Approve one claim at 390 px, one from an email link (a reused or expired link is refused), one from Slack and one from Teams test workspaces, and bulk-approve three; each records its channel and refuses a stale revision.

- **APPROVE-004 — Rule-based auto-approval.** The owner can let expenses that meet strict conditions approve themselves (for example matched card charges under $75 with a receipt and no exception), recorded as approved by rule with the rule version, and sampled by POLICY-003. **Acceptance evidence:** Enable the rule; see a compliant $40 matched charge approved by rule and a $40 charge with an exception routed to a person.

- **APPROVE-005 — Hold one expense, approve the rest.** On any approval step an approver can put one or more expenses on hold with a reason and approve the remaining expenses of the same revision in one decision. Coaster splits the claim: the approved expenses continue as their own revision along the workflow to batching and payment, and the held expenses move to a new claim that stays with the employee, carrying the hold reason, the approver and a link to the original claim. The employee answers the hold by fixing the expense (for example adding an attendee list) and resubmits the held claim on its own, or the approver releases the hold; held expenses are chased by FOLLOWUP-001 like any other open item and age in REPORT-002. Every COA-006 guarantee holds for both parts: the decision applies to the exact revision shown, nobody holds or approves their own expense, a stale view is refused, and the split and both claims' histories are append-only (COA-007). Holds also work from every APPROVE-003 channel and for card charges. Exact rules today: with one approval step, the approver holds inside an approve decision on the exact revision shown, with a required reason, and must approve at least one expense (holding every expense is a return). The approved expenses become the claim's next revision, approved at once and batched like any approved claim, while its earlier revision keeps every expense; the held expenses keep their ids, splits and attendees and move to a new claim returned to the employee, which names the original claim, the approver, when and why, with each receipt copied byte for byte (the original receipt stays with the original claim's earlier revision). The employee fixes and submits the held claim like any claim; until then the approver who held it may release the hold, which freezes the held claim as it stands as its first revision and approves it, submitted by whoever submitted the original. Payment is the finance-recorded payment of COA-009 until PAY-001. No multi-step workflows, reminders, ageing reports, chat or email approval, or card charges exist yet, so each clause that adds one carries this obligation before it ships: APPROVE-001 (a hold on any step), FOLLOWUP-001 (held expenses chased), REPORT-002 (held expenses aged), APPROVE-003 (holds from every channel) and FEED-004 (card charges held). **Acceptance evidence:** Submit a five-expense claim; as the approver hold the dinner with "Add the attendee list" and approve the other four; see the four reach a batch and be paid (PAY-001) while the dinner sits in a new claim with the employee, with the reason and a link to the original; advance the clock and see FOLLOWUP-001 remind the employee; add the attendees and resubmit the held claim alone and see it approved and paid separately. The employee cannot hold or approve their own claim, a hold sent against an older revision is refused, and history on both claims shows the split with who and when.

- **APPROVE-006 — Comments and questions without a decision.** Anyone who can see an expense or claim (employee, approver, finance, auditor, delegate) can comment on it or ask a question of a named person without approving, returning or holding it; the named person is notified (NOTIFY-001), can answer in the app or from a Slack, Teams or email reply, and open questions show on the approver's queue and are chased by FOLLOWUP-001. Comments are part of the append-only history (AUDIT-001) and are never editable after another reply. Exact rules today: a comment is on the claim or on one of its expenses and never changes the claim's status or version; a question names one person who can open the claim (its employee, its approver, finance, an auditor or the owner), never the asker, and stays open until that person or their delegate replies in Coaster. The named person is emailed through the installation's mail provider, and the answer to the request says whether the provider accepted the message or mail is not set up; nothing claims a send that did not happen. Each claim row counts its open questions, the approver's queue included. The author may edit a comment until anyone replies, the earlier wording stays in the history line, and comments are never deleted; a delegate comments in the granting person's name, recorded as theirs on that person's behalf (ADMIN-005). Answers from chat or mail replies, notification preferences and chasing do not exist yet, so each clause that adds one carries this obligation before it ships: APPROVE-003 and CAPTURE-006 take answers from Slack and Teams replies, CAPTURE-002 from email replies, NOTIFY-001 delivers by each person's chosen channel, FOLLOWUP-001 chases open questions, and APPROVE-005 holds and FEED-004 card charges take comments the same way. **Acceptance evidence:** As an approver ask "Which client was this for?" on one expense of a pending claim; see the claim stay pending with the question marked open, the employee notified, the employee answer from a Slack reply, and the approver approve with the answer visible; try to edit the question after the reply and be refused.

- **APPROVE-007 — Retract a submitted claim and unapprove before payment.** An employee, or their delegate (ADMIN-005), can retract a submitted claim before any approver has decided it, for example to add a forgotten receipt. The claim returns to draft, the retraction is recorded on that revision, it leaves every pending approver's queue and their reminders stop, and resubmitting makes the next revision (COA-007). Once any step has approved, retraction is refused and the employee asks the approver to return it. The approver who approved, finance or an admin can unapprove an approved claim with a reason until it is in a reviewed batch, released for payment (PAY-004) or posted into a closed ledger period. The claim goes back to the step that approved it, or to the employee, as the person unapproving chooses; anything ACCT-001's at-approval trigger already posted to the ledger is voided or reversed there; and the history keeps the original approval and the unapproval with who, when and why. After a batch is reviewed or released, unapproval is refused and corrections follow ACCT-006. Exact rules today: with one approval step, "before any approver has decided" means the claim is still waiting for its approver; the retraction is a history line naming the revision it ends, and the claim leaves the approver's queue. Until ADMIN-001 adds the admin role, the owner acts for "admin". "The approver who approved" is the person who recorded the approval or the approver it was recorded on behalf of (APPROVE-002, ADMIN-005). An approved claim can be unapproved while it is in no batch or in a draft batch not yet reviewed, and unapproving takes it out of that batch; once its batch is reviewed, unapproval is refused. Sent back to the approver, the same frozen expenses become the next revision for a fresh decision (COA-006); sent to the employee, the claim is returned for correction. No reminders, ledger postings, card charges, release step or ledger periods exist yet, so each clause that adds one carries this obligation before it ships: FOLLOWUP-001 stops a retracted claim's reminders, APPROVE-001 sends an unapproval back to the step that approved, ACCT-001, ACCT-002 and ACCT-003 void or reverse an at-approval posting on unapproval (card charges arrive with FEED-004), PAY-004 refuses unapproval once released, and ACCT-006 refuses it in a closed period. **Acceptance evidence:** Submit a claim and retract it before any decision; add a receipt, resubmit, and see revision 2 with the retraction recorded on revision 1 and the approver's pending reminder stopped; another employee's attempt to retract it is refused; retract a claim after its first step has approved and be refused. Approve a card-charge claim and see its purchase posted to the QuickBooks sandbox; unapprove it with a reason and see the claim back with the approver, the purchase voided in QuickBooks, and both events in history; try to unapprove a claim in a reviewed batch and be refused.

### Follow-up and automation

- **FOLLOWUP-001 — Reminders that chase people.** Coaster reminds employees about missing receipts, unsubmitted expenses, held expenses (APPROVE-005) and unanswered questions (APPROVE-006, AGENT-002), and approvers about pending decisions, on a schedule finance sets (for example after 2, 5 and 10 business days), through each person's chosen channels (NOTIFY-001), respecting their time zone, quiet hours and US federal holidays. Each reminder links straight to the one thing to do and stops the moment it is done. **Acceptance evidence:** With a clock-controlled installation, leave a receipt missing, a draft unsubmitted, a held expense unanswered and a claim unapproved; advance the clock and see reminders on the scheduled business days, none on a holiday or in quiet hours; resolve each and see its reminders stop.

- **FOLLOWUP-002 — Escalation with service levels.** When a reminder sequence is exhausted, the item escalates to the person's manager, then to finance, as finance configures; approval service levels (for example three business days) show on the queue and in reports (REPORT-002). Escalations are recorded on the item. **Acceptance evidence:** Let an approval pass its three-day service level and see escalation to the approver's manager and then finance, each recorded; approve it and see escalation stop.

- **FOLLOWUP-003 — Missing-evidence chasing on card spend.** Card charges without a receipt, memo or required field are chased automatically from the day they post, with the option to freeze an issued card (CARD-001) after a configured number of days and unfreeze it when the evidence arrives. **Acceptance evidence:** Post a charge with no receipt, advance seven days and see three reminders and the card frozen; attach the receipt and see the card unfreeze and the chase close.

- **NOTIFY-001 — Notification delivery and preferences.** Coaster sends notifications for submission, decision, return, payment, failure, reminder and escalation events by email (Base mail adapter with Postmark), Slack, Microsoft Teams, Nook and mobile push. Each person chooses channels per event type, digest or immediate; delivery attempts and provider acceptance are recorded per message, and a failed channel falls through to email. **Acceptance evidence:** Set one person to Slack-immediate and another to an email daily digest; generate events and see the Slack messages and one digest; make the Slack webhook fail and see the attempt recorded and the email fallback sent.

- **AUTO-001 — Automatic reports and scheduled submission.** Expenses group into reports automatically (by period, trip or card statement) and submit on a schedule the owner sets (daily, weekly, twice monthly, monthly) when they are complete and compliant; incomplete items stay behind with their reminders. **Acceptance evidence:** Set weekly submission; add five compliant expenses and one missing a receipt; at the scheduled time see a report of five submitted and the sixth held with a reminder.

### Reimbursement

- **PAY-001 — ACH reimbursement through a payment provider.** Finance releases an approved batch and Coaster pays each employee by ACH through Dwolla (or another provider adapter), from the company's verified funding account, choosing standard or same-day ACH. Each transfer is tracked through pending, processed, failed and returned states with the Nacha return code in plain words (for example R03 no account), failed amounts return to the employee's balance for repayment after correction, and employees see each state on their claim. COA-009's manual route remains for companies that pay elsewhere. **Acceptance evidence:** In the Dwolla sandbox, verify a funding account and two employees, release a batch, see both transfers settle and the claims marked paid; simulate an R03 return and see the failure, the plain-words reason, the employee notified and the amount back in their balance; retry after correction and see it settle once.

- **PAY-002 — Employee bank details and change protection.** Employees add their bank account through the provider's verification flow (instant account verification or micro-deposits); Coaster stores only the provider's token and the last four digits. A change of bank details notifies the employee at their previous contact, holds payments for a cooling-off period finance sets, and requires a fresh sign-in. **Acceptance evidence:** Add an account by instant verification and another by micro-deposits; read the database and archive and find only tokens and last four; change the account and see the notice to the old address, the payment hold and its release after the period.

- **PAY-003 — Payroll reimbursement route.** As an alternative to ACH, approved reimbursements go to the next payroll run in Gusto, ADP RUN, Paychex Flex or Rippling as non-taxable reimbursement lines (and non-accountable amounts as taxable wages, CLAIM-008), confirmed back from the payroll provider. **Acceptance evidence:** With the Gusto developer sandbox, send a batch to payroll and see reimbursement lines on the next run and the claims marked paid when the run is confirmed; a non-accountable amount appears as taxable.

- **PAY-004 — Payment controls and schedules.** Finance sets a reimbursement schedule (for example every Friday), dual release above an amount, step-up sign-in for release, and who may release; each release is recorded with who, when and the provider reference, and the batch total, provider totals and ledger clearing reconcile to the cent. Building on COA-009's reviewer rule, nobody releases a batch containing their own claim. **Acceptance evidence:** Set dual release over $5,000 and a Friday schedule; see a $7,200 batch wait for a second releaser, a releaser with their own claim refused, and after release batch, provider and ledger totals agree.

- **PAY-005 — Reimbursing employees outside the US.** Employees of non-US entities (ADMIN-004) or US-entity employees with foreign bank accounts are reimbursed in their local currency through an international payout provider adapter (Wise Business API by default; Canadian employees by EFT in CAD): finance releases them in the same batch flow under PAY-004 controls, the FX rate, fee and provider reference are recorded on each payment, and settlement and failure are tracked to the claim as for PAY-001. **Acceptance evidence:** In the provider's sandbox, add a Canadian entity employee's CAD account and a UK employee's GBP account; release a batch holding them and a US employee; see the US transfer go by ACH (PAY-001) and the CAD and GBP payouts by the international provider with rate, fee and reference recorded; fail one payout and see the claim reopened for payment with the reason.

### Accounting

- **ACCT-001 — QuickBooks Online two-way sync.** Coaster connects to QuickBooks Online by OAuth, pulls accounts, classes, locations, departments, customers, vendors and employees, and posts card expenses as purchases on the card account, reimbursable expenses as bills to the employee as vendor (or journal entries, as configured), payments as bill payments from the funding account, and every receipt as an attachment. Sync runs on its own, with nobody pressing a button. **Posting triggers** are set per record type: card and other non-reimbursable charges post as purchases at final approval; reimbursable expenses post as bills at final approval or, as finance configures, at payment (PAY-001 release or PAY-003 payroll confirmation); a bill payment posts when its ACH transfer is released or, as configured, when it settles, and is reversed if the transfer is returned (PAY-001). A posting job runs on every trigger event and at least every 15 minutes to retry anything outstanding. A **scheduled dimension refresh** pulls accounts, classes, locations, departments, customers, vendors and employees daily at a time finance sets; a manual "sync now" stays available to finance and the bookkeeper (ADMIN-001). **Employee vendors:** an employee with no vendor link is matched to an existing QuickBooks vendor by email, then exact name; when none exists and auto-create is on (the default), the vendor is created on first posting and linked to the person, and with auto-create off the posting waits in the ACCT-006 queue with a one-step "create vendor" fix. **Payment status comes back:** with "sync reimbursed bills" on (the default), each run reads payments recorded in QuickBooks against Coaster's bills. A bill fully paid in QuickBooks marks its claim reimbursed with the QuickBooks payment reference, date and paying account, removes it from any draft batch, and refuses it from any later batch or ACH release; a partial payment leaves the remainder owed. A payment made in Coaster marks the QuickBooks bill paid. Each record carries Coaster's ID, so re-sync updates instead of duplicating, and changes in QuickBooks (a deleted or edited entry, a deleted bill payment) are detected and shown. **Acceptance evidence:** On a clock-controlled installation against a QuickBooks sandbox company, with nobody pressing sync: (1) final-approve a card-charge claim and see the purchase on the card account, with its receipt attached, after the next posting run; (2) with the reimbursable trigger at final approval, final-approve a claim and see its bill posted; switch the trigger to at payment, approve another and see no bill until its ACH batch is released, then see the bill and bill payment posted together with totals equal to the batch; (3) post for an employee with no QuickBooks vendor and see the vendor created once and reused on their next claim, and with auto-create off see the posting queued with the create-vendor fix; (4) mark one Coaster bill paid inside QuickBooks and, after the next run, see that claim marked reimbursed with the QuickBooks payment reference, gone from the draft batch, and refused from a later batch and from an ACH release; (5) add an account and a class in QuickBooks and see both offered as choices after the next scheduled daily refresh; (6) re-run sync and see no duplicates; delete one bill in QuickBooks and see the discrepancy flagged; return an ACH transfer (PAY-001) and see its bill payment reversed.

- **ACCT-002 — Xero two-way sync.** The same for Xero, with ACCT-001's posting triggers, posting job, daily dimension refresh, employee contact matching and creation, and payment status coming back. Tracking categories, accounts and contacts are pulled on the daily refresh. Card spend posts as spend-money transactions on the card account at final approval; reimbursable expenses post as bills to the employee contact at final approval or at payment, as configured; payments are recorded on release or settlement; receipts are attached; every record is idempotent by Coaster ID. A bill paid in Xero (a payment recorded against it, including through a Xero batch payment) marks the Coaster claim reimbursed with the Xero payment reference and keeps it out of any later batch or ACH release. **Acceptance evidence:** Against a Xero demo company on a clock-controlled installation, with nobody pressing sync, repeat ACCT-001's journey: a final-approved card claim appears as a spend-money transaction after the next run; a reimbursable claim on the at-payment trigger posts its bill and payment when its ACH batch is released, with totals equal to the batch; an employee with no contact gets one created once; a bill paid in Xero marks its claim reimbursed with the Xero reference and is refused from a later batch and ACH release; a tracking option added in Xero appears after the next daily refresh; re-sync makes no duplicates.

- **ACCT-003 — Pantry sync.** For buyers on Pantry, Coaster posts the same records through Pantry's API with ACCT-001's posting triggers, posting job, vendor handling and payment status coming back. It receives Pantry's chart, dimensions, vendors and period locks on the daily refresh, and at once through Pantry's change webhooks where Pantry sends them, so Pantry's ledger and bank reconciliation see Coaster's clearing entries without manual matching. A bill Pantry records as paid marks the Coaster claim reimbursed with Pantry's payment reference and keeps it out of later batches and ACH releases. **Acceptance evidence:** With a Pantry test installation and a clock-controlled Coaster, with nobody pressing sync: final-approve a card claim and see the expense in Pantry after the next run; release an ACH batch on the at-payment trigger and see bills, payments and attachments posted with totals equal to the batch, and Pantry's bank-feed line for the ACH debit reconcile against Coaster's clearing entry; mark one bill paid in Pantry and see the claim marked reimbursed with Pantry's reference and refused from a later batch and ACH release; add an account in Pantry and see it in Coaster after the next refresh.

- **ACCT-004 — NetSuite and Sage Intacct.** For larger buyers, Coaster posts expense reports, vendor bills and card transactions to NetSuite and Sage Intacct with subsidiaries, departments, classes, locations and projects, idempotent by Coaster ID, with ACCT-001's posting triggers, daily dimension refresh, employee vendor handling and payment status coming back. **Acceptance evidence:** Against vendor sandboxes with nobody pressing sync, final-approve claims in two entities and see records in each subsidiary with dimensions and attachments after the next run; mark one vendor bill paid in NetSuite and one in Sage Intacct and see both claims marked reimbursed with the ledger's reference and refused from a later batch.

- **ACCT-005 — Exports alongside live sync.** Every batch, period and report exports as CSV and XLSX with configurable column templates (including QuickBooks Desktop IIF), so companies on any ledger can import, and exports name the receipts by hash and link. **Acceptance evidence:** Export a batch with the default and a custom template and as IIF; import the IIF into a fixture validator and see totals match the batch.

- **ACCT-006 — Posting integrity and close.** Sync errors (an archived account, a missing class) go to a queue with the record, the ledger's message and a one-step fix, and retry only what failed. A closed ledger period locks posted expenses in Coaster; a later correction posts a dated reversal and a new entry rather than editing the closed one. **Acceptance evidence:** Archive an account in the sandbox and see the failing records queued with the message; map a new account and retry only those; close March and correct a March expense in April, then see a reversal and new entry dated April.

- **ACCT-007 — QuickBooks Desktop connection.** For companies still on QuickBooks Desktop (Pro, Premier or Enterprise), Coaster connects through Intuit's QuickBooks Web Connector: the owner installs the generated connector file, and on each scheduled run Coaster pulls accounts, classes, customers, vendors and employees and posts card charges, reimbursable bills, bill payments and journal entries with the same idempotency and error queue as ACCT-001 and ACCT-006. IIF export (ACCT-005) remains available. **Acceptance evidence:** Against a QuickBooks Desktop test company (or a Web Connector qbXML fixture at the network edge), run the connector and see accounts and classes pulled; sync a batch and see the bill and card-charge requests with totals equal to the batch; re-run and see no duplicate; make one request fail and see it queued with the QuickBooks message and retried alone.

### Reporting and API

- **REPORT-001 — Spend analytics.** Finance and managers (within their scope) see spend by employee, department, category, merchant, project, card and period with drill-down to the expense and receipt, budget versus actual for every budget set under REPORT-004, and saved views. **Acceptance evidence:** Load six months of fixture spend; group by department and drill to a receipt; a manager sees only their reports' spend; set a department budget (REPORT-004) and see budget, actual and remaining for it in the grouped view; save a view and reopen it.

- **REPORT-002 — Operational and compliance reports.** Reimbursement aging, approval cycle time per approver, receipt compliance rate, unsubmitted card spend for month-end accruals, policy exception trends and feed coverage, each exportable and deliverable by scheduled email. **Acceptance evidence:** Run each report on fixture data and check its totals against the underlying records; schedule the accrual report for the last business day and see it delivered.

- **REPORT-003 — Searchable audit history and auditor access.** Every expense, claim, card charge, batch, payment and sync has a searchable history (who, what, when, from which channel or agent). An auditor role reads everything and changes nothing, and finance can export an audit pack for a period with receipts, decisions, exceptions and payment references. **Acceptance evidence:** Search history for one claim across capture, approval, payment and sync; sign in as an auditor and see writes refused; export a quarter's audit pack and verify receipt hashes against stored bytes.

- **REPORT-004 — Budgets with threshold alerts.** Owners, admins and finance set budgets by category, department, project, tag or the whole entity, monthly, quarterly or yearly, either shared or per member (for example $200 a month of meals per person), each with an owner and one or more alert thresholds (for example 80% and 100%). Actual spend counts card charges from posting and out-of-pocket expenses from submission, with approved and pending shown separately; a threshold crossing notifies the budget owner and finance once per period through NOTIFY-001, and an optional policy rule marks an expense that would exceed its budget as a soft exception for the approver (POLICY-001). Budgets are versioned, import from CSV and appear in REPORT-001 and the AGENT-007 spend answers. **Acceptance evidence:** Set a $5,000 monthly Travel budget for Sales owned by the Sales head with 80% and 100% alerts, and a $200 monthly per-member meals budget; post $4,100 of Sales travel and see exactly one 80% alert to the Sales head and finance and the report show $5,000 budget, $4,100 actual (82%) split into approved and pending; post past $5,000 and see the 100% alert and the over-budget soft exception on the next claim; exceed one member's meals budget and see only that member flagged; advance to next month and see actuals reset with no repeat alert.

- **REPORT-005 — Claim PDF with receipts.** Anyone who can see a claim can download it as one PDF: a summary page (employee, period, totals by category, approval chain with who and when, payment reference), one line per expense with coding, exceptions and explanations, comments, and every receipt image or PDF page in expense order, each stamped with its expense and receipt hash; finance can download a batch of claims as one file, and billable expenses can be filtered to one customer for rebilling (CLAIM-006). **Acceptance evidence:** Download a paid three-expense claim with a JPEG, a HEIC and a two-page PDF receipt; see the summary totals equal the claim, the approval chain and payment reference, every receipt page present in order with its hash matching the stored bytes; download the customer-filtered PDF and see only that customer's billable expenses; a user who cannot see the claim is refused.

- **API-001 — Public API and webhooks.** A documented REST API with scoped API keys and OAuth covers expenses, receipts, claims, cards, batches, payments, people and reports, and signed webhooks announce state changes, so Crank and the buyer's own systems can automate around Coaster under the same role rules as the screens. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a scoped key, list and create expenses, be refused outside the scope, subscribe a webhook and verify its signature on a claim approval and a payment settlement.

### Administration

- **ADMIN-001 — People, roles, departments and the permission matrix.** Roles are owner, admin, finance, card admin, approver, employee, auditor (REPORT-003) and external bookkeeper, combinable per person (a person's powers are the union of their roles); people belong to departments with a manager hierarchy. Each role's powers are exactly the row in the role permission matrix below, enforced in every API handler (COA-008), agent route and chat or email action, not only in screens; every action outside a role's row is refused with no write. Separation holds across combined roles: nobody approves their own expense (COA-006), and release still follows PAY-004. Deactivation hands open approvals and unsubmitted card charges to a named successor before access ends. **Acceptance evidence:** Add one person holding only each role and a three-level hierarchy; a matrix test drives every action in the matrix through its public route as each single-role person and sees each permitted cell succeed and every other cell refused with 403 and no write (for example the bookkeeper exports and syncs but is refused releasing a payment, changing policy and reading bank details; the card admin freezes a card but is refused approving a claim or releasing a batch); a person with finance and approver roles gets both rows but cannot approve their own claim; deactivate a manager with open approvals and see them moved to the successor and the manager's access end at once.

### Security, privacy and data custody

- **AUDIT-001 — Append-only history of every action.** Every write (settings, claims, decisions, batches, payments, card controls, provider connections, syncs, agent actions) appends a numbered history line with actor, agent, channel and time that no route can edit or delete, and that survives export and restore. **Acceptance evidence:** Perform one action of each kind through its public door, read the history and see each line in order; try to alter or delete a line through every route and see refusal; export, restore and see identical history.

- **ERASURE-001 — Personal-data erasure with financial-record retention.** The owner can erase a former person's personal data (name, contact, bank tokens, chat and mailbox links, personal-card connections) while keeping the financial records the law requires, with the person replaced by an opaque subject in history and exports; an erasure receipt lists what was removed and retained, and a restored older archive cannot bring the erased data back. **Acceptance evidence:** Erase a departed employee with paid claims; see their personal fields gone, amounts and receipt hashes kept under an opaque subject, the receipt listing both; restore an archive made before the erasure and see the erasure re-applied.

- **RETAIN-001 — Record retention and legal hold.** Receipts and expense records are retained for a period the owner sets (seven years by default, matching common US record-keeping practice), never silently removed; expiry needs owner confirmation and leaves a receipt, and a legal hold on a person, entity or period blocks expiry and erasure until released. **Acceptance evidence:** Set a short retention on a test installation and see expiry proposed, confirmed and receipted; place a legal hold and see expiry and ERASURE-001 refused until it is released.

- **SEC-001 — Financial data and provider credential custody.** Card numbers and bank account numbers are never stored, only provider tokens and last four; provider credentials and OAuth tokens are encrypted at rest, excluded from exports and named in the restore checklist; payment release, bank changes and provider connection need a recent sign-in, with passkey or SSO step-up where configured. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect each provider, export the installation and scan the archive and database for card and account numbers and tokens (none in clear); attempt a payment release with a stale session and see step-up required.

- **DATA-001 — Declared external data flows.** `seed.json` declares each outbound data flow (Cloudflare hosting, mail, receipt reading, Plaid, card programs, the payment provider, ledgers, HRIS, chat, travel and mailbox connections) with the data sent, before that flow ships; optional providers stay off until the owner connects them, and the core claim flow works with none connected. **Acceptance evidence:** With no providers connected, run the claim-to-batch journey and observe no outbound traffic beyond Cloudflare and the configured mail adapter; connect each provider and see only its declared data leave.

- **A11Y-001 — Accessible screens.** Every screen meets WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard operation, visible focus, labeled controls, sufficient contrast and screen-reader announcements for state changes such as reading progress and payment status. **Acceptance evidence:** Run axe on every main screen at 1440 and 390 px with no violations, complete the claim and approval journeys by keyboard only, and check reading and payment state announcements with VoiceOver.

- **COST-001 — Visible provider costs in USD.** The owner sees what each connected provider costs per use (Plaid per connected account, ACH per transfer, receipt reading per page, SMS per message) and month to date in USD before and after enabling it, with optional caps that pause the optional service rather than the core claim flow. **Acceptance evidence:** Enable receipt reading with a $20 monthly cap and read the CAPTURE-003 labeled corpus; see the per-page and month-to-date cost recorded against the corpus run; read receipts until the cap and see reading pause with manual entry still working.

## Push further with agents

Every agent here acts through the COA-010 authority model: it works for a named person or for finance under standing rules the owner grants, every action is recorded in AUDIT-001 with the agent named, and money movement, policy changes and accusations stay with people.

- **AGENT-001 — The spend agent closes every expense.** For each card charge and out-of-pocket purchase the agent works the whole path until receipt, coding, approval, reimbursement settlement and ledger entry agree: it reads the receipt (CAPTURE-003), asks the employee in Slack or Teams for exactly the missing context, applies coding (CODE-002), routes approval (APPROVE-001), prepares the payment run and posts to the ledger, chasing the responsible person or system under finance's cadence. Finance sets the standing rules, escalation owners and which steps it may take alone; payment release, changed bank details and disputed coding always need a person, and the agent stops on any dispute. **Acceptance evidence:** Post 30 sandbox charges with mixed receipts; see the agent collect receipts and context by chat, route approvals and prepare a payment run that a person releases; see every step in history with the agent named, a disputed coding stop the item, and an attempt to release payment refused. Over the CODE-002 coding fixture set, the coding the agent applies meets CODE-002's bars: 100% on merchant-rule charges and at least 90% category matches on repeat merchants.

- **AGENT-002 — Resolve unclear receipts with one question.** Where the reading, the merchant's email receipt and the card charge disagree (tip, tax, currency, business purpose), the agent asks the employee one specific question with the receipt crop and the conflicting values, applies the confirmed answer, and keeps the original reading and the source crop. It never invents a receipt, fills a value nobody confirmed, or changes a paid claim. **Acceptance evidence:** Submit a receipt whose total differs from its card charge by a handwritten tip; see one question with the crop and both values; confirm and see the corrected expense with the original reading retained; ignore the question and see no value changed. Over the CAPTURE-003 labeled corpus matched to fixture card charges, every receipt where the reading and the charge disagree gets exactly one question and none gets a value the label contradicts.

- **AGENT-003 — Preview a policy change before adopting it.** The agent finds the exceptions that recur (the same rule broken by many people for the same reason, routinely approved) and proposes the policy changes that would remove them; finance can also propose its own change (a new limit, threshold or auto-approval rule). For each candidate the agent replays it against the last 12 months of claims, card spend, exceptions and approval delays and shows which employees and expenses it would affect, the change in exceptions, reimbursements and approver workload, and the recurring exceptions it would remove. Finance inspects the affected expenses and adopts a new policy version (POLICY-001) or discards it; past decisions are never re-judged. **Acceptance evidence:** On fixture history where a $50 meal limit is routinely exceeded and approved, see the agent surface that recurring exception and propose raising the limit to $75; see affected claims, exception count before and after and approver hours saved; adopt it and see a new version effective from today with history unchanged.

- **AGENT-004 — Repair feed and accounting handoffs.** The agent compares feed cursors, statement totals, payment references and ledger postings, finds gaps (a missing day of transactions, a payment not cleared, a bill missing in QuickBooks), opens a reconnection or mapping task for the right person and reprocesses only confirmed gaps once fixed. It never treats a missing feed as zero spend, issues a second payment or posts a duplicate. **Acceptance evidence:** Drop a day from a sandbox feed and delete a synced bill in QuickBooks; see the agent identify both with evidence, open the reconnect and resync tasks, and after reconnection reprocess only the gap with no duplicates.

- **AGENT-005 — Policy review agent with cited reasons.** Before an approver opens a claim, the agent reviews each expense against the policy version, receipt, card data, trip and past spend, and writes a recommendation with the rule and evidence cited. Within an envelope the owner grants (for example matched, compliant spend under $100) it approves on the owner's behalf; everything else goes to the approver with the recommendation. Approvers see how often they agreed. **Acceptance evidence:** Grant a $100 compliant envelope; submit a claim with a compliant $60 lunch and an over-limit $180 dinner; see the lunch approved by the agent with cited reasons and the dinner routed with a recommendation; revoke the envelope and see the next compliant lunch routed to a person. Over the CODE-002 coding fixture set, every expense the agent approves within its envelope carries coding equal to its label or its merchant rule, and every expense whose coding came from a low-confidence suggestion is routed to the approver with that coding marked for review.

- **AGENT-006 — Fraud and anomaly investigation.** The agent investigates POLICY-002 signals across employees, cards and time (repeated near-duplicates, edited receipts, split charges, merchants unusual for a role) and assembles an evidence pack for finance with the images, charges and pattern. It never contacts the employee about a suspicion, blocks payment or concludes wrongdoing; finance decides and records the outcome. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed a pattern of split charges and an edited receipt across two employees; see one evidence pack with the related records; confirm no message was sent to the employees and no payment was held until finance acts.

- **AGENT-007 — Conversational expenses and spend questions.** An employee tells the agent in Slack, Teams, the app or by text "add my Denver trip" with photos, and it builds the claim with receipts, per diem and mileage for review before submission. Finance and managers ask spend questions in plain words ("what did Sales spend on travel in Q3 compared with Q2?") and get answers computed from Coaster's records, with links to the underlying expenses and limited to what the asker may see. **Acceptance evidence:** In Slack send four receipt photos with "Denver client trip, Oct 6–8" and see a draft claim with receipts, per diem and a question for the missing purpose; as a manager ask a Q3 versus Q2 question and see figures that match REPORT-001 with links; ask about another department and see the scope refusal.

- **AGENT-008 — Month-end close preparation.** On the close schedule the agent lists unsubmitted card spend and pending claims for accrual, chases their owners (FOLLOWUP-001), checks card statements reconcile (FEED-005) and that every paid reimbursement has cleared in the ledger, and hands finance a close checklist with an accrual entry ready to post. Finance posts the accrual. **Acceptance evidence:** On a fixture month with unsubmitted charges and one uncleared payment, see the checklist with the accrual amount by department, the chases sent and the uncleared payment named; post the accrual and see it in the QuickBooks sandbox.

- **AGENT-009 — Trip assembly.** From travel bookings (INTEG-001), the employee's connected calendar and card charges, the agent groups a trip's expenses, fills per diem days and locations and mileage legs, and proposes one complete claim for the employee to confirm. **Acceptance evidence:** With a Navan itinerary, a calendar event and eight charges, see one proposed trip claim with the right expenses, per diem days at the city's GSA rate and an airport mileage leg; the employee edits and submits it.

## Honest boundaries

- Coaster is not a bank, card issuer or lender. Company cards are issued by the issuing partner and its sponsor bank (CARD-001), which hold the funds, run business verification and own credit decisions; Coaster runs the controls and the expense workflow. Existing cards from any issuer stay usable through feeds. Commercial card feed enrollment (FEED-002) is completed with each issuer on the issuer's timetable and forms; Coaster supplies the delivery endpoint, keys and checklist and tracks each step, and statement import covers any days before the first file arrives.
- Coaster does not hold reimbursement funds or operate ACH. Dwolla (or another provider) and its bank partners move the money under Nacha rules, with their own onboarding and timing; Coaster owns approval, release controls, status and reconciliation.
- Coaster never asks for or stores bank sign-in credentials. Feeds come through Plaid and card program files, institution coverage is shown, and statement import is the recovery path; no feed is promised to be universal or instant.
- Coaster does not run payroll or file taxes. Payroll stays with Gusto, ADP, Paychex or Rippling (PAY-003); 1099 filing, taxability and deductibility decisions stay with the buyer's accountant or Pantry; Coaster supplies flags and records, not tax advice.
- Coaster is not a travel agency. Booking stays in Navan, TravelPerk, SAP Concur Travel or Egencia, and Coaster takes their itineraries and receipts (INTEG-001).
- Coaster is not the ledger, the procurement system, the HR record, the PSA or the chat tool: those are Pantry, Purse, Willow, Gauge and Nook (or the buyer's QuickBooks Online, Xero, Procurify, BambooHR, Harvest, Slack or Teams), connected through SIBLING-001 and the named integrations.
- The US is the first market: USD, IRS and GSA rules, US holidays and US providers. Foreign-currency receipts and additional entities are supported (CLAIM-003, ADMIN-004); foreign VAT reclaim is done by a specialist reclaim partner from Coaster's exported receipts.
- Native app store distribution, provider applications (Plaid, Dwolla, issuing partner, QuickBooks, Xero, Slack, Teams) and card program enrollment are engineering and partnership work tracked by their clauses, not cuts in scope. No SOC 2 report, PCI scope reduction or hosted service level is claimed without its own evidence.
- No agent verdict replaces a person's decision to release money, change a policy or act on a fraud signal, and raw records stay readable without any AI summary.

## Decided defaults

- Money is integer minor units; each mileage amount rounds half up once; the rate used is copied onto the expense.
- A missing receipt above the threshold is a soft exception that needs an explanation, unless finance makes it hard (POLICY-001). Exceptions never block approval on their own: the approver decides.
- "Exact duplicate" means the same receipt bytes, or for an expense without a receipt the same employee, date, type, merchant and amount; the same card transaction ID for card spend. Same date, merchant and amount with different receipts is a visible possible duplicate, not a block.
- One finance person may create and review a batch unless it holds their own claim; dual release applies above the PAY-004 threshold.
- Receipts and their history are never deleted outside RETAIN-001 and ERASURE-001.
- Agent keys and provider credentials are per installation, stored hashed or encrypted, and excluded from exports; a restored installation reconnects its providers and issues new keys.
- An archive carries Coaster's data, not sign-in accounts or sessions: roles are keyed by email, so after a restore people sign in to the new installation and keep their roles.
